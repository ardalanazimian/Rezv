### RT-01 — HOLDS, and the CEO's framing is too kind to the product

**The claim is true.** Verified three ways, chosen so that no two share a failure mode:

1. **Both naming conventions at once** — `cb(Base|Vip|Preorder|Winback)Pct` *and* `cb_(base|vip|preorder|winback)_pct`, across `.ts .mts .tsx .sql .prisma .mjs`. The camelCase-only grep is the one that would have missed a raw-SQL consumer.
2. **By concept, not by name** — every `/ 100` in all of `api/src`. If a percentage is applied to money anywhere, under any identifier, it appears here. Exactly **two** sites do: `coupons.ts:32` (coupon percent) and `reservations.ts:613` (cashback, `cbBasePct`).
3. **By consumer** — where `RESTAURANT_SELECT` (`staff-helpers.ts:28`, which selects all four) actually flows.

**Where the CEO's framing falls short.** He wrote: *"a non-zero default is the product asserting a
number to the person paying us."* It is not a default. It is a **product surface, built and shipped**:

- `api/src/app/api/v1/restaurant/cashback/route.ts` — `GET` + `PATCH`, zod-validated (0–50), RBAC-gated on `canManageSettings`, persisting all four percentages.
- `apps/business/js/staff-system.js:269` — **four sliders**, one per percentage, in a «کش‌بک» tab.
- `:273-276` — a **preview panel** that tells the owner what each guest type receives: «رزرو معمولی» · «با پیش‌سفارش» · «مشتری VIP» · «بازگشت».
- `:399-402` — on save, a success toast and a notification: «کش‌بک به‌روز شد».

Three of those four rows can never pay a toman. The owner moves a slider to «مشتری VIP ۲۰٪», the
server accepts it, the panel confirms it saved, and `reservations.ts:612` reads `cbBasePct` and
nothing else. **The gap between what the product promises the owner and what it pays the guest is a
UI, a validated endpoint, a database column and a success notification wide.** The remediation is
therefore not "change a default to zero" — it is a decision about a shipped feature, and it is the
CEO's to make, not mine.

Worth noting *in the product's favour*, because a red team that only reports damage is not measuring:
the checkout path itself is careful. Cashback is computed on `final` — the amount after discount,
i.e. what was actually paid (`reservations.ts:611`) — not on `subtotal`. That is the honest choice
and someone made it deliberately.

### RT-02 — a closed fix that covers one path and not its neighbour

`staff-system.js:242` seeds the panel with hardcoded percentages before any server call:

```js
let CB={base:8,pre:12,vip:18,wb:20};
```

`rCashback()` at `:245` guards its server read with `if(!_cbLoaded && API.getToken())`. The fix
recorded in the comment at `:250-256` is real and it is good — a 403 or a network failure now renders
an explicit «تنظیمات کش‌بک بارگذاری نشد» error state with a retry button, instead of silently
showing invented numbers as if they were the restaurant's settings.

**But the guard has two ways to be false, and the fix only handles one.** When `API.getToken()` is
falsy the whole block is skipped — no request, no error state — and the render falls through to the
hardcoded `CB`. The owner sees «پایه ۸٪ · پیش‌سفارش ۱۲٪ · VIP ۱۸٪ · بازگشت ۲۰٪» presented exactly as
saved settings are presented.

**Those four numbers match nothing.** The schema defaults (`schema.prisma:175-178`, and identically
`migrations/0_init/migration.sql:90-93`) are **5 · 8 · 12 · 20**. The panel's fallback is
**8 · 12 · 18 · 20**. So they are not even a stale copy of the defaults — three of the four are
numbers with no origin anywhere in the system. The comment's own words for why this matters are the
right ones: «مبنای تصمیمِ مالیِ غلط».

Severity depends on whether the no-token render is reachable in production, which I have not yet
established — the panel has an offline mode (`res.offline` at `:411`), which suggests it is. **I am
not upgrading this past PARTIAL until I have measured that**, and I am recording the limit rather
than rounding it up.

### RT-03 — lead: the isolation runs may have been force-exit runs

`api/package.json` defines two ways to run tests:

```
"test":     "tsx --test --test-reporter=spec tests/_all.runner.mts"
"test:one": "tsx --test --test-force-exit"
```

`test:one` — the natural command for "run this one test in isolation" — carries
**`--test-force-exit`**. That is attack #12 in the catalogue: force-exit is precisely the flag that
can turn a hang or an unresolved async assertion into a reported success. The CEO declared
`slot-lock-failopen-double-booking` a flake because it "passed 7/7 in isolation". If those isolation
runs went through `test:one`, then the green that exonerated the test came from the runner most
likely to manufacture one.

**I have not yet established which command was used**, so this is a lead, not a finding, and the CEO
should not act on it as though it were. Next step is to reproduce both ways on the same commit and
compare. Separately, the CEO has already conceded the deeper point: isolation removes the contention,
so isolation cannot be the disproof. If it fails only under load, load is the test.
