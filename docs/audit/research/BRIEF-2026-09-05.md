## What changed in the market this batch

- **SnappFood's loyalty mechanic is now documented, and it changes how confident we should be about
  proposal 001.** Snapp Club — SnappFood's group-wide points program — already runs exactly the
  "single, predictable, non-tiered expiry clock" pattern proposal 001 argues for (a flat pool, no
  tiers, resetting twice a year on a fixed calendar date). This means proposal 001 isn't just "avoid
  Starbucks/Chipotle's mistakes" — it's *also* "match the incumbent norm Iranian users already expect,"
  which is a stronger, lower-risk argument than the original global-benchmark framing alone.
- **A second, larger, independent Iranian data-trust incident surfaced**, alongside Fidilio's already-
  documented 2024 controversy: SnappFood confirmed a partial breach (Dec 2023/Jan 2024, 20M+ users,
  claimed by a group calling itself IRLeaks) and separately, Iran's Competition Council ruled (May
  2025) that SnappFood tied commission discounts to restaurant exclusivity — an anticompetitive,
  opacity-driven commercial practice, not a technical bug. Together with Fidilio, this is now **two**
  independent Iranian food-platform data/trust incidents in roughly the same 12-18 month window,
  strengthening proposal 002's premise beyond a single anecdote.
- **Servme confirmed the "flat SaaS, no per-cover fee" pattern holds even in the closest regional
  comparable to Iran's market** — reinforcing proposal 003's "publish real pricing, no lock-in" stance
  with a MENA-specific precedent, not just a Western one (OpenTable/SevenRooms).
- **New: Servme's explicit "built for the region, not bolted on" positioning** (Ramadan/Iftar-aware CRM
  tagging, WhatsApp-native messaging as a first-class product surface) is a genuinely new strategic
  idea this batch didn't have before — see proposal 004.

## The one thing you don't know yet

**Servme's own public materials contradict themselves on two separate facts (revenue/funding status,
and customer count), and I flagged both rather than picking a side** — this is worth knowing not
because Servme itself matters much to Rezervno's roadmap, but because it's a reminder of how much
"competitor data" in this space is unaudited, aggregator-sourced, and self-contradictory even for
companies with real products and real customers. When this repo's own CEO or research pipeline cites a
competitor "fact" from a single source, treat single-sourcing as a yellow flag by default, the same way
Scout's own profiles do — Fidilio's 4.9-vs-3.7 rating gap, Servme's 2,500-vs-3,000+ customer claims, and
its bootstrapped-vs-four-investors contradiction are now three separate examples of the same underlying
lesson inside two research batches.
