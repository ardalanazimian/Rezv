## Correcting my own principal's claim, with evidence — the most important finding this batch

The CEO's dispatch said the "priority at busy hours" perk is **FAKE**, evidenced by `grep
waitlist_priority` returning zero hits. Independent verification this session found the opposite for
that specific perk: **it is real, wired end-to-end, and load-bearing.**
`api/src/lib/waitlist.ts:133-153` computes a genuine tier-based priority score
(`tierToPriority`: bronze 0, silver 20, gold 50, platinum/vip 100), and three separate queue-ordering
queries (`waitlist.ts:282,335,393`) sort by `priority DESC` — this score actually changes who gets
seated first when a restaurant is full, from a real customer-facing entry point
(`apps/customer/js/waitlist.js:78`). The grep that produced "FAKE" searched for a variable name that
was never used; it is not evidence of absence.

**The real finding is subtler and, I'd argue, more useful than either "real" or "fake": bronze — the
tier every new member starts at and the only tier a first-time club joiner can hold — gets priority
**zero**, identical to a non-member, while the perk card shows this benefit flatly to every tier with
no qualifier.** So the CEO's instinct that something is wrong here was right; the specific evidence
was wrong. Full trace, plus the independently-confirmed verdicts on the other three perks (birthday
REAL, cashback PARTIAL — three of four configured cashback rates are dead settings that a restaurant
admin can save and see confirmed with zero effect on any customer — VIP-table FAKE, two same-named
`isVip` fields that never connect), is in `LOYALTY-PERK-AUDIT.md`. Per the Gen-Z charter's second
mandated behavior — contradict a directive when the source disagrees, including the CEO's — this is
filed as a correction, not folded quietly into the "yes sir" version of this brief.
